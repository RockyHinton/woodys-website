# Counting methodology

**This is the most important section in the file.** Every number below depends on it.

## The corpus counted

**53 Allen-directed works**, namely:

| Group | Count | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Theatrical features directed | **50** | 1966–2023; the list is in [`films.md`](films.md) |
| Anthology segment | **1** | "Oedipus Wrecks" in *New York Stories* (1989) |
| Television film | **1** | *Don't Drink the Water* (1994) |
| Television series | **1** | *Crisis in Six Scenes* (2016), counted as one work, not six episodes |

## Inclusion rules applied

**Included**
- Credits on any of the 53 works above.
- Both principal and significant supporting performances.

**Excluded**
- Films Allen wrote or acted in but did not direct (*What's New Pussycat?*,
  *Play It Again, Sam*, *The Front*, *Antz*, *Fading Gigolo*, etc.). These are listed in
  [`films.md`](films.md) §*Significant Other Screen Work* and are noted in individual
  records where they matter, but **they are not in the counts**.
- *Wild Man Blues* (1997), which Barbara Kopple directed.
- The untitled 2026 Madrid feature, which has no completed credits.
- Theatre and book collaborators, except where noted in *Other Key Collaborators*.
- Uncredited and single-shot cameo appearances.

## How to read the counts

- **A plain number** (e.g. "13") means every title is enumerated in the PROJECTS column
  and the arithmetic is checkable.
- **"at least N"** means N titles are confirmed in this dossier and further credits are
  likely but unverified. Treat the figure as a floor, not a total.
- `[CHECK]` on a specific title means that title's credit is unconfirmed; the count
  excludes it unless stated.

## Known divergence from published figures

Published figures for **Juliet Taylor** (casting) and **Carlo Di Palma**
(cinematography) differ from the counts here because other sources use different corpora
— see those entries. This is expected and is why the methodology is stated.

## Notes on the principal acting relationships

**Mia Farrow — 13 works, 1982–1992.** The largest acting collaboration of the career, and
the only one that spans an entire decade of consecutive films. It begins with
*A Midsummer Night's Sex Comedy* (released 1982-07-16) and ends with *Husbands and Wives*
(released 1992-09-18), which was in cinemas as the partnership publicly ended. For any
timeline or relationship visualisation, these two dates are the boundaries. Her mother
**Maureen O'Sullivan** plays the sisters' mother in *Hannah and Her Sisters*; her sister
**Stephanie Farrow** appears in two films; her apartment at **The Langham, 135 Central
Park West** is Hannah's apartment. See [`locations.md`](locations.md).

**Diane Keaton — 7 Allen-directed works, 1973–1993.** The relationship begins not on film
but on Broadway, in *Play It Again, Sam* (opened **1969-02-12**), and continues through
the 1972 film of that play, which Allen wrote but did not direct. Within the directed
corpus it runs *Sleeper* → *Love and Death* → *Annie Hall* → *Interiors* → *Manhattan*,
then a cameo in *Radio Days* (1987) and a return to the lead in
*Manhattan Murder Mystery* (1993) — a part written for Mia Farrow and recast after 1992.
Keaton won the Academy Award for Best Actress for *Annie Hall*. Allen appeared in the
TNT tribute *AFI Life Achievement Award: Diane Keaton* in 2017.

**Dianne Wiest — 5 works, 1985–1994.** The only performer to win **two** Academy Awards in
Allen films (*Hannah and Her Sisters*, 1986; *Bullets Over Broadway*, 1994) — and one of
only two actors with two acting Oscars from a single director's work in general `[CHECK]`.

**Wallace Shawn — 6 works, 1979–2020.** The **longest acting span in the filmography: 41
years**, from a small part in *Manhattan* to the lead in *Rifkin's Festival*. For
cross-film discovery this is the most useful single actor thread available.

**Judy Davis — 5 works, 1990–2012.** Academy-nominated for *Husbands and Wives*; also BAFTA
and Golden Globe nominated. A 22-year span.

**Scarlett Johansson — 3 consecutive works, 2005–2008.** Entirely within the European
period, and arrived on *Match Point* as a late replacement for Kate Winslet.

## Notes on the producing relationships

**Jack Rollins and Charles H. Joffe** are the structural fact of Allen's career. Rollins
became his manager around **1960** and suggested he perform stand-up; Joffe joined as
manager-producer; and between them they produced or executive-produced the films for the
next five and a half decades. **Rollins's last credit is *Irrational Man* (2015), and he
died that June** [S020] — **a 55-year professional relationship, the longest in the
dossier**, and the single most significant collaborator fact in the file.

**Letty Aronson** is Allen's younger sister [S022]. Her first producer credit is
*Mighty Aphrodite* (1995); from *The Curse of the Jade Scorpion* (2001) she is the
constant producer through *Coup de Chance* (2023), and she is a producer on the 2026
Madrid film. She also **co-produced *Bullets Over Broadway The Musical*** on Broadway in
2014 [S051] — making her the only person in this file credited on an Allen film, an Allen
television series and an Allen stage musical.

**Robert Greenhut** produced nineteen Allen works across two decades, 1977–1996, including
every film of the Mia Farrow period. He was nominated for the Academy Award for Best
Picture as the producer of *Hannah and Her Sisters* and won the Golden Globe for it.

**The handover is clean and datable:** Greenhut's last producer credit is
*Everyone Says I Love You* (1996); Jean Doumanian produces 1997–2000; Letty Aronson takes
over from 2001. Three producing eras, two transitions, both in the late 1990s.

## Notes

**Gordon Willis (8 films, 1977–1985)** defines the look of the mature New York period:
*Annie Hall*'s long static masters, the black-and-white widescreen of *Manhattan*, the
Academy-nominated period forgery of *Zelig*. He won the New York Film Critics Circle award
for *Zelig*. He died in 2014.

**Carlo Di Palma (11–12, 1986–1997)** covers the entire Mia Farrow late period and the
mid-1990s. He had shot for Antonioni before Allen. He also executed the deliberate
handheld break in *Husbands and Wives* and *Manhattan Murder Mystery*, and won a Nastro
d'Argento for *Shadows and Fog*. He died in 2004.

**Sven Nykvist (4, 1988–1998)** is the most significant single hire in the career's
critical history: Bergman's cinematographer, shooting the films for which Allen was most
often compared to Bergman.

**Vittorio Storaro (5, 2016–2023)** shot the last five features, beginning with Allen's
**first digital film**. His saturated, shifting colour in *Wonder Wheel* is the most
discussed photography of the late period.

**The published-count divergence.** Secondary sources give Di Palma **11** or **12**
Allen films [S042]. Both are right: 11 theatrical features, 12 credits including the 1994
television film. This dossier states both.

(*Take the Money and Run*, *Bananas*, *Everything You Always Wanted…*, *Sleeper*,
*Love and Death*, *Annie Hall*, *Interiors*) + Morse 20 + Lepselter 22 = **50**. ✓

## Notes

**Ralph Rosenblum (1969–1978)** is credited in the standard accounts with restructuring
*Annie Hall*'s assembly into its non-linear memory form, and with persuading Allen to
change the endings of both *Take the Money and Run* and *Bananas* `[CHECK]`. He won a BAFTA
for *Annie Hall* with Wendy Greene Bricmont.

**Susan E. Morse (1979–1998)** edited twenty consecutive features, from *Manhattan* to
*Celebrity* — **the second-longest single-discipline tenure in the career**. She was
Academy-nominated for *Hannah and Her Sisters* and BAFTA-nominated for *Radio Days*.

**Alisa Lepselter (1999–present)** has edited **every** Allen film since *Sweet and
Lowdown* — 22 features and the television series — making hers **the longest continuous
single-discipline collaboration in the filmography**, now in its twenty-seventh year.

**The one clean handover point:** *Celebrity* (1998) is Morse's last and
*Sweet and Lowdown* (1999) is Lepselter's first. The same film is also Sven Nykvist's
last. See *The successions* below.

**1998–1999 is the great discontinuity of the career.** Within one year Allen lost or

new. Any visualisation of the career's craft history should mark this.
