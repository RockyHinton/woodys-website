# Woody Allen Master Research Dossier

**RESEARCH LAST UPDATED: 2026-11-02**

Internal factual reference for the rebuild of **woodyallen.com** as a modern digital
archive of Woody Allen's complete body of work.

---

## 1. Purpose

This dossier is the project's single coordinated factual reference. It exists so that
future development sessions can populate the website without re-researching the career
from scratch, and without guessing.

It is written for two readers:

1. a human editor preparing publishable content;
2. an AI coding agent that needs to locate a specific fact quickly, months from now.

It is **not** a biography, not an essay collection, and not a runtime data source.

**On the biographical controversy:** the long-running allegations against Allen, and his
response to them, are *career facts* that affect distribution history (notably
*A Rainy Day in New York*, *Rifkin's Festival* and *Apropos of Nothing*). They are
recorded here only where they explain a distribution, publication or production event.
This dossier takes no position on them and does not research them as a topic. Editorial
policy for the website is a separate decision, not a research output.

## 3. Research methodology

Research was carried out live against web sources on **2026-11-02**, in this order:

1. Establish the canonical directed filmography from first-party material
   (woodyallen.com/filmography) and cross-check against reference databases.
2. Characterise the legacy official site as an archival source — including its defects.
3. Research films systematically (core record, credits, cast, locations, awards).
4. Research non-film career work: books, theatre, television, audio, jazz.
5. Research chronology and exact dates.
6. Research locations, separating story setting from confirmed filming location.
7. Derive collaborator relationships and counts from the researched film records.
8. Verify quotations; discard anything unverifiable.
9. Calculate statistics from this dossier's own dataset, with stated methodology.
10. Consistency pass across files (names, dates, titles, source IDs).

### Source preference order applied

1. Official Woody Allen / Gravier Productions material (woodyallen.com)
2. Original film credits and published screenplays
3. Official distributor / studio press materials (press-kit text republished on the official site)
4. Film festival archives
5. Academy / BAFTA / major award bodies
6. Institutional archives (AFI Catalog, Fundación Princesa de Asturias, IBDB)
7. Reputable major publications (*Variety*, *Deadline*, *The Hollywood Reporter*, *The Bookseller*, *Playbill*)
8. Established film reference databases
9. Published interviews and books
10. Secondary reference sources

### Verification standards

- **Nothing in this dossier is invented.** Where a fact could not be established, the
  record says so.
- Where credible sources disagree, both readings are recorded and the disagreement is
  logged in [`sources.md`](sources.md) under *Conflicts*.
- Story settings are never recorded as filming locations. See
  [`locations.md`](locations.md) §1.
- Quotations are held to a higher bar than any other category. A small verified set is
  worth more than a large plausible one. See [`quotes.md`](quotes.md).

### ⚠️ Known limitation to carry forward

A substantial share of per-film **credits, runtimes and release dates** in
[`films.md`](films.md) derives from cross-checked reference encyclopedias rather than
from primary end credits. This is adequate for building and laying out the site. It is
**not** adequate for publication-sensitive fields. Before any film page goes live,
confirm the Core Record and Principal Creative Credits blocks against:

- the film's own end credits, and/or
- the AFI Catalog record (institutional, title-by-title), and/or
- the official site's own press-kit text where it exists.

This is logged as research gap **G-CREDITS-01** in [`sources.md`](sources.md).

A numbered master register lives in [`sources.md`](sources.md). Identifiers are used
inline throughout the dossier:

```

```

Citations are applied to facts that are **publication-sensitive, dated, contested or
first-party** — not to every sentence.

### Research flags

| Flag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `[VERIFIED]` | Strong evidence, usually first-party or institutional. Used sparingly, only where it adds information. |
| `[CHECK]` | Likely correct, but should receive manual confirmation before publication. |
| `[UNVERIFIED]` | Could not be established reliably from available sources. |
| `[CONFLICT]` | Credible sources disagree; the disagreement is documented in `sources.md`. |

Unflagged records are cross-checked but not primary-source confirmed. Treat the
limitation in §3 as applying to them.

`YYYY-MM-DD` where a precise date is known. `YYYY-MM` or `YYYY` where it is not.
Never a precise date invented to look complete.

## 5. Concise career overview

Allan Stewart Konigsberg, born **1935-11-30** in the Bronx, New York City [S022], began
selling jokes to newspaper columnists and Broadway comics as a teenager, and was writing
professionally for television by **1955**. He moved to stand-up in **1960** under
managers Jack Rollins and Charles H. Joffe, and released three live comedy albums
between 1964 and 1968.

His first produced screenplay was *What's New Pussycat?* (**1965**); his first
directing credit was the re-dubbed *What's Up, Tiger Lily?* (**1966**); his first
wholly original film as writer-director-star was *Take the Money and Run* (**1969**).

*Annie Hall* (1977) won four Academy Awards including Best Director and Best Original
Screenplay. From that point Allen sustained an almost exactly annual production rhythm
for four decades — unusual in modern cinema — moving between comedy, chamber drama,
formal experiment (*Zelig*), ensemble film (*Hannah and Her Sisters*) and moral thriller
(*Crimes and Misdemeanors*, *Match Point*).

From **2005** the work became substantially European: London (*Match Point*, *Scoop*,
*Cassandra's Dream*, *You Will Meet a Tall Dark Stranger*), Barcelona
(*Vicky Cristina Barcelona*), Paris (*Midnight in Paris*, *Coup de Chance*), Rome
(*To Rome with Love*), the French Riviera (*Magic in the Moonlight*) and San Sebastián
(*Rifkin's Festival*).

*Coup de Chance* (2023), his first film in French, is his **50th** feature [S033]. A
**51st**, set and shot in Madrid, entered production on **2026-10-05** and remains
untitled [S036][S038].

Across the career he has also published humour collections, a memoir and a first novel;
written for Broadway and off-Broadway; directed one opera production; and played
New Orleans–style jazz clarinet publicly for roughly half a century.

Totals and their methodology are in [`statistics.md`](statistics.md).

## 6. Important research caveats

1. **Credits provenance.** See §3. The single most important outstanding task.
2. **The legacy official site is evidence, not truth.** woodyallen.com is a first-party
   archive of real value — it hosts screenplay scans, international poster artwork,
   galleries, cast material and full press-kit production notes. It also contains
   demonstrable errors: a film listed with the wrong title, a credit attributed to the
   wrong person, nine films whose links are dead placeholders, one film linking to
   another film's page, and image `alt` attributes containing AI-tool error strings.
   All documented in [`sources.md`](sources.md) §*Legacy site audit*.
3. **Story setting ≠ filming location.** Rigorously separated. Several famous "New York"
   sequences were shot in New Jersey; several "European" interiors were shot in New York
   studios.
4. **Award-body year conventions differ.** Academy Award rows are given by *film year*,
   not ceremony year, unless stated. BAFTA and Golden Globe tables in secondary sources
   mix both conventions; one such internal inconsistency is logged as a `[CONFLICT]`.
5. **Time-sensitive facts are dated.** Anything about the current film, current
   publication or current status carries an explicit date. Do not promote these into
   permanent historical statements without re-checking.
6. **Collaboration counts are derived, not cited.** They are computed from this
   dossier's own film records using a stated inclusion rule. Changing the rule changes
   the number. See [`statistics.md`](statistics.md) §1.
7. **Quotations are deliberately sparse.** Most widely circulated Woody Allen quotations
   trace back to aggregator sites with no source. Those are excluded on principle.

## 8. How Future Development Agents Should Use This Dossier

**Read this section before writing any code.**

1. **These files are reference material. They are not runtime website data.**
   Nothing here is a schema, a fixture, or an API response.

2. **Application components must never parse these Markdown files as a database.**
   Do not `fs.readFile` them, do not glob them, do not write a Markdown-to-JSON build
   step over `/research`. If you find yourself writing a parser for this directory, stop
   — you are solving the wrong problem.

3. **Verified information is transferred into structured production content under
   `/content`,** authored as TypeScript records conforming to the interfaces in
   `lib/types.ts` (`Film`, `CastMember`, `FilmingLocation`, `Quotation`,
   `ArchiveItemRef`, …). That transfer is a **deliberate, per-record editorial act** —
   a human or agent reading a dossier record and writing a content record. It is not a
   bulk conversion.

4. **Carry the flags across.** A `[CHECK]` fact must not silently become published
   prose. Either verify it first, or leave the field out — every optional field in
   `lib/types.ts` is optional precisely so that a record can ship with gaps.

5. **Retain sources for publication-sensitive facts.** Exact dates, awards, locations,
   quotations, book publication details and anything time-sensitive should keep a
   traceable link back to a `[Sxxx]` identifier in whatever form the content layer
   supports (a comment in the content record is sufficient and is the current
   convention).

6. **Prefer absence to invention.** An empty `locations` array is correct. A plausible
   invented address is a defect that will outlive you.

7. **Update the dossier when you learn something.** If a primary source resolves a
   `[CHECK]` or a `[CONFLICT]`, amend the dossier record *and* the entry in
   `sources.md`, and bump the date in §*RESEARCH LAST UPDATED* above. Do not leave the
   knowledge only in the content layer.

8. **Do not expand the dossier's scope on your own initiative.** The deferred topics in
   §2 are deferred on purpose.
