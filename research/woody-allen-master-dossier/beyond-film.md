# Books & Writing

## Overview

Allen's published work falls into five groups:

1. **Humour collections** — short pieces, most originally published in *The New Yorker*,
   collected in five volumes between 1971 and 2022.
2. **Autobiography** — one volume, 2020.
3. **Fiction** — one novel (2025) and one collection of long stories (2026).
4. **Compilations** — two omnibus reissues of the humour collections.
5. **Interview books** — authored *about* Allen, not by him, but central to the
   bibliography because they are the main source of his recorded statements on his own work.

**Important caveat on the official site.** The Other Projects page lists only six books:
*Zero Gravity*, *Mere Anarchy*, *Woody Allen on Woody Allen*, *Side Effects*,
*Without Feathers* and *Getting Even* [S003]. It **omits** *Apropos of Nothing* (2020)
and, in that list, *What's With Baum?* (2025) — although *Baum* is promoted prominently
on the home page with retailer links [S001]. The official book list is therefore
incomplete and must not be treated as the canonical bibliography `[VERIFIED]`.

## Getting Even

- **Title:** Getting Even
- **Publication year:** 1971
- **Exact publication date:** not established `[UNVERIFIED]`
- **Publisher:** Random House `[CHECK]`
- **Type:** Humour collection — short prose pieces
- **Concise original description:** Allen's first book. Seventeen short comic pieces, most first published in *The New Yorker*, including parodies of philosophical treatises, detective fiction, intellectual biography and the memoir form.
- **Nature of contents:** Short satirical prose, 2–8 pages each; several are formal parodies rather than narratives.
- **Significant context:** Appeared two years after *Take the Money and Run* and in the same period as *Bananas* — the literary and film careers begin in parallel. ISBN 0-394-47348-5 [S023].
- **Related works:** Collected with *Without Feathers* and *Side Effects* in *The Complete Prose* (1991) and *The Insanity Defense* (2007).
- **Current official/publisher link:** listed on the official site's Other Projects page [S003].
- **Sources:** [S003], [S023]

## Without Feathers

- **Title:** Without Feathers
- **Publication year:** 1975
- **Exact publication date:** not established `[UNVERIFIED]`
- **Publisher:** Random House `[CHECK]`
- **Type:** Humour collection, **including two one-act plays**
- **Concise original description:** A second collection of comic prose, taking its title from Emily Dickinson's "hope is the thing with feathers". Alongside the parodies it prints two complete one-act plays, *God* and *Death*.
- **Nature of contents:** Short prose pieces, mock journals ("Selections from the Allen Notebooks"), and the two plays.
- **Significant context:** **Bibliographically the most important of the humour volumes**, because *Death* is the direct source of the film *Shadows and Fog* (1991) and *God* has an independent performance history. ISBN 0-394-49743-0 [S023].
- **Related works:** *Shadows and Fog* (film, 1991); see §*Theatre* below.
- **Sources:** [S003], [S020], [S023]

## Side Effects

- **Title:** Side Effects
- **Publication year:** 1980
- **Exact publication date:** not established `[UNVERIFIED]`
- **Publisher:** Random House `[CHECK]`
- **Type:** Humour collection
- **Concise original description:** Seventeen further comic pieces, including "The Kugelmass Episode", in which a professor is magically inserted into *Madame Bovary*.
- **Nature of contents:** Short prose; the pieces are generally longer and more narrative than in the earlier two volumes.
- **Significant context:** "The Kugelmass Episode" won the **O. Henry Award** `[CHECK]` and anticipates the device of *The Purple Rose of Cairo* (1985) — a character crossing between fiction and reality. A documented prose-to-film lineage worth recording. ISBN 0-394-51104-2 [S023].
- **Related works:** Issued separately as a chapbook, *The Lunatic's Tale* (1986), ISBN 1-55628-001-7 [S023]. *The Purple Rose of Cairo* (film, 1985).
- **Sources:** [S003], [S023]

## Mere Anarchy

- **Title:** Mere Anarchy
- **Publication year:** 2007
- **Exact publication date:** not established `[UNVERIFIED]`
- **Publisher:** Random House
- **Type:** Humour collection
- **Concise original description:** Eighteen comic pieces, most first published in *The New Yorker* after 1980, satirising contemporary American affectations — wellness industries, celebrity publishing, property speculation, consultancy.

## Apropos of Nothing

- **Title:** Apropos of Nothing
- **Publication year:** 2020
- **Exact publication date:** **2020-03-23** `[CHECK]` [S027]
- **Publisher:** **Arcade Publishing**, New York [S023][S027]
- **Type:** Autobiography
- **Concise original description:** Allen's single autobiographical volume, covering his Brooklyn childhood, the joke-writing and stand-up years, his managers, the films, his marriages, the jazz band, and at length the allegations and litigation of the 1990s.
- **Nature of contents:** Continuous first-person narrative, not a collection.
- **Significant publication history — the central fact about this book:**
  - **Grand Central Publishing (Hachette Book Group)** announced the book with a publication date of **2020-04-07** [S027].
  - Hachette dropped it following staff protests [S027].
  - **Arcade Publishing** then published it, reportedly with little advance notice, on
    **2020-03-23** `[CHECK]` [S027]. Arcade is generally described as an imprint of
    Skyhorse Publishing; that relationship was **not confirmed** in this research pass
    `[UNVERIFIED]`.
- **Why it matters for the website:** this is a dated, documented publication event and
  belongs in the timeline and News archive as such. It is also the chief first-party
  source for Allen's own account of his life — and therefore a **verification route for
  biographical facts and quotations**, as noted in [`quotes.md`](quotes.md).
- **Not listed on the official site's book list** [S003] `[VERIFIED]`.
- **Related works:** *Zero Gravity* (2022), also Arcade.
- **Sources:** [S003], [S023], [S027]

# Theatre

## Overview

Allen's theatrical career runs from a 1960 Broadway revue to a 2014 Broadway musical, and
divides into:

- **Two Broadway hits as playwright (1966, 1969)**, both of which became films;
- **One Broadway full-length play (1981)**;
- **One-act plays**, some published in *Without Feathers* (1975), some staged off-Broadway
  in the 1990s and 2000s;
- **One Broadway musical book (2014)**, adapted from his own film.

The official site's Other Projects page lists eight theatre entries [S003]; the list is
usable but incomplete (it omits, for example, the published one-acts and the opera
production).

---

## Don't Drink the Water

- **Title:** Don't Drink the Water
- **Year:** 1966
- **Type:** Full-length comedy, two acts
- **Writer:** Woody Allen — his first Broadway play
- **Director:** Stanley Prager `[CHECK]`
- **Venue/premiere:** **Morosco Theatre**, Broadway; the production later transferred to other houses [S028] `[CHECK]`
- **Opening date:** **1966-11-17** [S028] `[VERIFIED]` — two independent sources agree
- **Closing date:** **1968-04-20** [S028] `[CHECK]`
- **Principal cast:** Lou Jacobi (Walter Hollander), Kay Medford, Anita Gillette `[CHECK]`
- **Concise description:** An American caterer and his family, holidaying behind the Iron Curtain, are mistaken for spies and take refuge in the US embassy, where the ambassador's incompetent son is in charge.
- **Production history:** A substantial Broadway success running roughly seventeen months. Licensed for stage performance by Concord Theatricals, so the text remains in print and available [S029]. One Concord page gives the opening year as 1968, apparently conflating it with the closing date `[CONFLICT]` [S029].
- **Film relationship:** **Filmed twice.** A 1969 theatrical feature directed by Howard Morris with Jackie Gleason (Allen uninvolved), and Allen's own 1994 television film. See [`films.md`](films.md).
- **Major revivals/adaptations:** widely performed in regional and amateur theatre through its licensing agent [S029].
- **Significance:** **High.** The property with the longest multi-media life in the whole career: play (1966) → film by another hand (1969) → Allen's own film (1994) → continuing stage licensing.
- **Sources:** [S003], [S020], [S028], [S029]

## Play It Again, Sam

- **Title:** Play It Again, Sam
- **Year:** 1969
- **Type:** Full-length comedy
- **Writer:** Woody Allen
- **Director:** Joseph Hardy `[CHECK]`
- **Venue/premiere:** **Broadhurst Theatre**, Broadway [S028]
- **Opening date:** **1969-02-12** [S022][S028] `[VERIFIED]` — multiple sources agree
- **Closing date:** **1970-03-14**, after **453 performances** [S028] `[CHECK]`
- **Principal cast:** **Woody Allen** (Allan Felix), **Diane Keaton** (Linda Christie), **Tony Roberts** (Dick Christie), Jerry Lacy (Humphrey Bogart) `[CHECK]`
- **Concise description:** A film critic deserted by his wife is coached in seduction by the imagined ghost of Humphrey Bogart, and falls for his best friend's wife.
- **Production history:** 453 Broadway performances. IBDB records a further run from **1970-09-15 to 1971-02-27**, which the available sources do not explain — possibly a touring or return engagement `[UNVERIFIED]` [S028]. Still widely licensed and performed [S049].
- **Film relationship:** Filmed in **1972**, directed by Herbert Ross, with Allen adapting his own play and the stage trio of Allen, Keaton and Roberts reprising their roles. See [`films.md`](films.md).
- **Significance:** **Very high.** This is where Allen first worked with Diane Keaton and Tony Roberts — the beginning of two of the most important acting relationships of his career, and the reason the *Annie Hall* and *Manhattan* ensembles exist. The Broadway opening date, **1969-02-12**, is therefore one of the most significant single dates in the career.
- **Sources:** [S003], [S020], [S022], [S028], [S049]

# Television

## Overview

Television is where Allen's professional career began — as a staff and freelance gag
writer in the 1950s, years before his first film. It then recurs sporadically: topical
specials in the 1960s, a suppressed political satire in 1971, one television film in 1994,
and one streaming series in 2016.

The official site's Other Projects page lists **fourteen** television entries [S003],
which is the most useful single inventory available, though several of its items could
not be independently verified.

# Stand-Up & Audio

## Stand-up career

- **Began:** **1960**, at the urging of his manager **Jack Rollins**, who suggested Allen
  perform the jokes he had been writing for others [S022].
- **Professional stage debut:** **1960-10**, at the **Blue Angel** nightclub, Manhattan
  [S022] `[CHECK]`.
- **Principal venues:** Greenwich Village clubs including **The Bitter End** and
  **Cafe Au Go Go**; later the Blue Angel and the Village Gate `[CHECK]` [S022].
- **Active period as a performing stand-up:** **1960–1969** [S022].
- **Managers:** **Jack Rollins** and **Charles H. Joffe** — who would then produce his
  films for the next fifty-five years, until Rollins's death in 2015. See
  [`collaborators.md`](collaborators.md).
- **Television exposure:** *The Tonight Show* from **1963-11-01**; *What's My Line?*
  (1963–1967); a UK special, *The Woody Allen Show*, 1965 [S020][S022].
- **Material:** first-person monologue built on anxiety, failure, marriage, intellectual
  pretension and physical cowardice — the register that became the screen persona. The
  albums are the primary record.

## Performance history and touring

- **Michael's Pub (1970s–late 1990s).** Allen's Monday-night residency at Michael's Pub in
  Manhattan is the foundation of the musical career. Sources disagree on when it began —
  1971 in one account, the mid-1970s in another `[CONFLICT]` [S041]. **Eddy Davis joined
  Allen at the Monday residency in 1985** [S041].
  - Film crossover: additional recording for the *Sleeper* (1973) score was reportedly done
    at Michael's Pub `[CHECK]` [S020] — a direct documented link between the residency and
    the films.
- **Café Carlyle (1997 onward).** After Michael's Pub closed, the band moved to the Café
  Carlyle, playing **Monday evenings from 1997** whenever not on tour [S041]. The
  engagement was deliberately low-profile: no advertising, no press invitations, audience
  by word of mouth [S041].
  - Film crossover: the Café Carlyle appears **in *Hannah and Her Sisters* (1986)**, where
    Mickey takes Holly to hear **Bobby Short** — eleven years before Allen's own band took
    the same room [S030][S041]. One of the best cross-domain facts in the dossier.
  - Documentary crossover: *Always at the Carlyle* (2018), in which Allen appears [S020].
- **Touring.** The band's **first European tour was in 1996**, with sold-out dates
  including **the Olympia in Paris** and **the Royal Festival Hall in London** [S041]. It
  returned to Europe repeatedly and toured the United States several times, including a
  **West Coast tour in 2015** [S041].
- **Pause and resumption.** The March 2020 closure ended the residency. A **2023 European
  tour** was described as ending the longest break in Allen's musical career [S041]. No
  further public appearances as a musician have been reported `[CHECK]`.

## Relationship between the music and the films

Four distinct patterns, all documented in [`films.md`](films.md):

1. **Compiled jazz and standards as score.** Allen's default method: no original score,
   period recordings instead. *Annie Hall*, *Stardust Memories*, *Radio Days*,
   *Crimes and Misdemeanors*, *Café Society* and most of the rest.
2. **Jazz as subject.** *Sweet and Lowdown* (a fictional 1930s guitarist),
   *Sleeper* (his own band's score), *Wild Man Blues* (himself).
3. **Deliberate non-jazz exceptions**, which define the rule: Gershwin in *Manhattan*,
   Mendelssohn in *A Midsummer Night's Sex Comedy*, Kurt Weill in *Shadows and Fog*,
   Caruso and Verdi in *Match Point*, Spanish guitar in *Vicky Cristina Barcelona*,
   and **Philip Glass's commissioned score** for *Cassandra's Dream* — the only original
   orchestral commission between 1972 and 2026.
4. **Original music by working jazz musicians.** Dick Hyman (from *Zelig*, 1983) and
   Stephane Wrembel (*Midnight in Paris*, *Rifkin's Festival*).

**Only three original scores are documented across fifty features:** Marvin Hamlisch
(*Take the Money and Run*, *Bananas*), Mundell Lowe (*Everything You Always Wanted to Know
About Sex*), and Philip Glass (*Cassandra's Dream*) — plus Dick Hyman's original songs and
scores, which sit between pastiche and composition. This is a strong By-the-Numbers item;
see [`statistics.md`](statistics.md).

## A note on register

Avoid romanticising this material. The documented facts are: Allen plays clarinet in a
traditional New Orleans idiom; he held a weekly Monday residency in New York for roughly
five decades; the engagement was unadvertised; the band was led by a professional banjoist;
and the music recurs through the films as compiled repertoire rather than as original
composition. That is a substantial and interesting record on its own terms and does not
need embellishment. Claims about his standing as a musician should be attributed to named
sources or omitted.
